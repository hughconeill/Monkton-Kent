# Stuart's Research

**A folk history of Monkton, by Stuart Horsburgh**

The main focus of this research is on Monkton Village 100 years ago – 1926. So
only the buildings present at that time are researched here in a little
detail. Some villager stories, newspaper articles and photos take us into the
latter part of the 20th century.

This village is ideally situated between St Augustine’s landing place at
Ebbsfleet in AD 597, and his founding in the same year of Canterbury
Cathedral, the principal seat of the Archbishop of Canterbury who leads the
Church of England and the worldwide Anglican Communion.

When Queen Eadgiva in AD961 gave the fertile land consisting of marsh,
pasture, arable and forest to the monks of the Cathedral, the village of
Monkton (or Monocstun, Monkynton – “The Monks’ Land”) began its journey as a
place rich in farming history.

Today, in 2026, the free village magazine, “Monkton News”, is delivered to 324
households, and the latest ‘Census’ in 2024 estimated a population of 841
residents. So I’d like to take you back about 100 years when there would be
just approximately 100 households and 420 residents, to visualise the scene
you’d see if you walked down Monkton Street in those pre-World War 2 days.

My research began during the summer of 2025, when I put on paper everything
that I could decipher from the 257 gravestones in the ancient 12th century
churchyard of St Mary Magdalene Church in Monkton, in Thanet. I produced a
book which can be read on this website (see the
[churchyard survey](../../church/churchyard/index.md)), as well as in the
churchyard (during the dryer summer months). But whilst I did simple gardening
in the churchyard as a volunteer, visitors showed interest in the book because
they had a connection with the village in some way. I heard their stories
about life in Monkton from their own experiences, and I wondered if it was
possible to collect this type of information, to give present and future
generations a taste of what life had been like, living here in the early part
of the 20th century in a very agricultural setting.

Therefore, from a combination of official census documents, parish council
publications, books and histories of the village, photo collections of
postcards and memorabilia, and a lot of personal memories I have collected
from current and ex Monkton residents, here is the result.

```{admonition} Please help!
:class: tip

If the accuracy of this information is in some way defective, then my
apologies, as I have faithfully tried to document all that I have gathered.
I’m afraid that census clerks have got a lot to answer for in their
legibility and accuracy!

I’ve only lived here since 1983 myself, so I’m of a later generation and I
didn’t have a personal connection to the village before then. I’m hoping that
from the telling of this story, you, dear readers, may be able to enjoy it,
appreciate it, recall your own reminiscences, and pass them on to us! It’s a
‘Folk History’, so please add to it, and I’m always willing to note your
facts, figures and photos that will give us a more accurate understanding of
what it was like to live here in the 20th century. Contact details are on the
[About](../../about.md) page.
```

I thank you for your time and interest. And I celebrate our villagers for
their contribution to the ‘Story of Monkton Village’.

*Stuart Horsburgh*

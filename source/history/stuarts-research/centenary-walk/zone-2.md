It later became the “White Stag” in the 1960’s, and briefly in recent times the “Olive Tree”, “the Stag”, and then “The New Stag” – and in 2026 it is presently closed. Originally a small beer house from after the Napoleonic Wars (1815). The Duke of Wellington’s Beer Act of 1830 enabled any householder, for just 2 guineas a year (£2 5p : – : a guinea=21 shillings), to retail only beer between 4am and 10pm. It was naively believed that these ‘Tom & Jerry’ shops would wean the social drinker from the rotgut of spirits to ‘wholesome’ beer. In practice tipplers became just as soaked on beer as they ever did on gin, and ‘beer shops’ acquired a reputation as disorderly houses. In 1872 it was formerly named “The New Inn”.

The pub has belonged to a succession of breweries including Flints of Canterbury, Fremlins, and Shepherd Neame of Faversham.

In 1949, Fremlins bought out George Beer & Rigdens in Faversham, but that brewery closed in 1954. In the early ‘50’s, Fremlins would deliver from Faversham using a Foden Steam engine dray. It could only be used on certain routes, where water was available for the engine!

When Herbert MILLS, took over as licensee of the “New Inn” pub in 1951, Fremlins the brewers informed him that horses had the right of way through the bar to the back of the premises, where they were stabled. No side entrance existed then, being flanked on one side by “Forge House” and the other by the old “Forge” itself, where the blacksmith Fred Harrison shod most of the horses of the area.

Bert IRELAND would sing “A Farmers Boy” in the bar. There was a Winter Darts Team.

During Brian STEPHENSON’s ownership 1972 – 1985, he put in the “Space Invaders” video arcade game, invented in 1978, that attracted a lot of interest and custom, at 5p per go.

Brian instigated a lot of sympathetic modernisations to the pub, including inside toilets, accommodation rooms upstairs, making the left side suitable as a local’s bar, and the right side a lounge. Also added in the late ‘70’s was the restaurant extension at the side and rear.

It was a busy and popular pub, with coach parties from Blue Rambler Coaches of Cliftonville and other coach Countryside Tours.

The “White Stag” pub sign was also a popular question for car treasure hunt rally quizzes to drive to and find :- (e.g. “When is a stag a turkey?!)

Jerry RUSH was the resident pianist / organist, playing for busy Saturday evenings 7pm – 10.30pm; and just as busy for the Sunday lunchtime trade from 1200 till 2pm (the old opening hours in the ‘70’s!) There were crowds queueing at the door, and for ‘health & safety’ reasons there may have been ‘extended’ opening hours occasionally! It was always popular with the nearby “Foxhunter Park” clientele.

But the Monkton villagers were serious customers too, for example, ‘on-the-dot regulars every day for pints of light and bitter – Doug STANNARD, Sidney ‘Snowy’ SMITH, Fred OSBOURNE, and Charlie WOODCOCK. Ray HISCOKE from Seamark Close was provided with his special choice of ‘Aschback German brandy’ – and when a German lorry driver came into the pub having got lost, and speaking no English, Ray was on hand to speak to him in fluent German learnt in a WW2 POW camp! ‘Kitch’ DUNK was the cellarman; ‘Flossy O’NEILL was the barmaid (and she was very fond of animals, lived at the Foxhunter Park, and died in 2026 aged 99 years!).

There were teams for darts and pool, especially in winter; a bat & trap pitch out the back in summer; and cards and dominoes games for indoors.

Brian remembered a blizzard in the mid ‘70’s that had stranded a full coachload on the top A299 road. They were walked down across the fields and settled in the pub for the night with the fires burning.

The local constabulary, Ken JONES from Monkton, and Bob SMITH from Minster (named ‘Smith & Jones’ after the TV programme!) kept a friendly eye on the pub.

## Left: “Forge” / ‘Smithy’ (demolished)

In the present car park of “The White Stag” pub, on the right-hand side, old photographs show that here was the site of the ‘Smithy’, where Fred Harrison in the 1950’s was the renowned farrier and blacksmith – a very important person in an agricultural village where horses were kept for ploughing, leisure riding, pulling carts, and transport to nearby towns. Farm and cart tracks came before the tarmac roads we experience today.

Albert BURTON operated the “Forge” with the help of various good tradesmen e.g. Mssr McNALLY, father & son; Ted BINGHAM; J. APPLETON; and Fred HARRISON.

In a 1966 Isle of Thanet newspaper article on Monkton, Fred HARRISON says : “I still shoe about 40 horses a month, although these are now mainly riding horses as opposed to the old-fashioned cart-horses”.

When Drake & Fletcher built their engineering business opposite the pub and smithy, a smithy was built at the rear of their premises, and the original smithy demolished in favour of the pub car park you see now.

1934 Kelly’s Directory - lists Albert Henry BURTON as blacksmith.

George VAUGHAN (b1877) worked the forge and later was taken on by his brother-in-law.

There was no gap between the pub and the smithy, so horses needing to get to their stables behind the pub had a ‘right-of-way’ through the pub bar! Eric MILLS remembers his father, licensee Herbert MILLS, being told this by Fremlins in 1951.

## Right: “Drake & Fletchers” (demolished)

The buildings were opposite the pub and established in the early 1950’s. They were a successful Maidstone based company of engineers and farming services - agricultural engineers. A Smithy was built at the back of the business, and blacksmith Fred Harrison moved there when his Smithy next to the pub opposite, was demolished. They lost their dealership contract with John Deere and closed down soon afterwards. Then it was bought by DOWNFAST DEMOLITION and later sold for a housing development.

Shop first mentioned in the 1881 census, run by grocer and master baker John LAMB. In 1882 Kelly Directory John LAMB is listed as Post Office receiver. John LAMB was the 1st Parish Council chairman from 1894-99. He died in 1900, is buried in the Monkton churchyard, and he has a plaque in the Methodist Chapel for his service.

When Percy JARVIS shouted at some robbers in his Shop one time, it was heard in the pub next door, and they all came out running to catch the thief!

Bertha DUNK remembers in 1989 that Alfred & Amy McMULLEN (c 1920’s) were both bakers and would bake the bread and cakes in the on-site bakehouse at the back.

Village Postman was Mr SETTERFIELD. He walked the deliveries twice a day. Perhaps this was the time before the buildings were numbered, and only the cottage names were used? Just a few people left in the village now who remember their address by its cottage name. I can’t find out when the houses in Monkton Street became numbered rather than named – perhaps the 1960’s? The Postal Code system was completed in 1974.

Mr DAVIES helped at the Post Office. Also a Telephone Exchange, manually operated, and if you were out he’d take a message for you and phone you with it later (Good Service, and before the modern era of ‘Voicemail’!)

Eric BLADES (1937-2004) was given the job of ringing a “3 minute bell” throughout the village….. to warn of an impending nuclear war! We can all sleep well in our beds tonight now the government has taken over this role from Eric!

The Village Shop and Post Office closed in 1997 and is now a private house – but the letterbox still remains embedded into the wall!

When it did close, Bernard COLE took over the responsibility for the Post Office, from his building premises next to the original Post Office at #4 “Delce Cottages”. It was certainly a place to get stamps etc. on certain days of the week.

The Monkton Post Office and Shop was used by the famous local model railway firm, Hornby Hobbies of Margate, as a design for their own New R487 00 Gauge Country Post Office Model Kit-Town & Country Series (it was priced £16.15p on eBay recently!)

When the caravan site was started in the early 1950’s by Harry RADCLIFFE, the first caravan to be installed there was by Mrs FORD, whose late husband was the Postmaster General of Peru! During the years after WW2, caravan parks met a growing demand for cheaper holidays and were very popular.

The site used to be a FULLER’S Farm field, and WW1 Peace Celebrations were held there – old villagers remembering a meal, races, and being given a ‘Peace Mug’.

In an Isle of Thanet Gazette newspaper article – 25-Feb-1966 – “Let’s take a look at…..MONKTON” there is a photo of Keith IRWIN, the manager of “Foxhunter Park” – “responsible for the 220 holiday caravans and the 26 residential vans which are parked there. Mr Irwin has about 800 visitors staying on the site, many of whom come from London”.

Site manager Graham LEWIS was part of the London Caravan Group – who also had a site at Walton on the Naze in Essex. There is a strong connection between Foxhunter and south London. There was room for 360 units when the trees were removed in the 60’s / 70’s.

The 30 acre site was bought by Colin SAUNDERS in 1977/79. It had a green double decker 1952 Routemaster bus that took site residents free to local towns and markets – it ended up in Las Vegas?

Popular animals at the Park were Harvey the Cockatoo (1980-2005), Ringo the flamingo, 2 macaws that flew about the village and would attack dogs being walked on the “Drove”. Also peacocks that often paraded down Monkton Street or in nearby gardens, including mine!

It had a £1m Mediterranean water feature garden with koi carp added in the ‘80’s, that was opened by entertainers Don Estelle and Windsor Davies from the TV series “It Ain’t Half Hot Mum!”

There was a 1998 Channel 4 reality TV programme called “Holiday Park” that was an intriguing insight into the Park and its owner Colin Saunders. The Park also featured in a Channel 4 tv show “Four in a Bed” in October 2015 with a couple that lived on the Park.

## Left: “Walters Hall” (demolished in the late 1940’s)

```{note}
It used to stand where semi-detached bungalows #92 & #94 are now.
```

Before coming into the ownership of Viscount Molesworth, it was once in possession of the QUEKES, from whom it passed to the CRISPE family, and was afterwards held by Lord HOWARD of Effingham, who disposed of it in 1723. The present house was built by James Ruck.

- **1903 Kelly’s Directory** – “Walters Hall” is the residence of G.A. COLLETT.
- **1911 census** – Viscount George Bagot MOLESWORTH (1867 – 1947) born in Paris, retired, The 9th Viscount Molesworth. He married Nina Alida Faulkner on 6-Feb-1894. Viscountess Nina MOLESWORTH nee Faulkner (1865-1958) born in Madras India. Also daughter Cecily MOLESWORTH (1903-1986 – born in Higham, Kent) - (2/3 children elsewhere.) Also servants Edith FORD (b1861) & Florence HATTON? (b1892). 18 rooms.

*By 1939 they lived in Chorleywood, Hertfordshire. Sophie, the present Duchess of Edinburgh, married to Prince Edward, is a direct descendent of the 1st Viscount Molesworth (1656-1725) through her grandmother.*
- **1913 Kelly’s Directory** – “Walters Hall” is the residence of Viscount MOLESWORTH.
- **1915** – Kathleen HARMES nee Rogers was born here. Went on to marry Tom HARMES.
- **1939 census** – the space where “Walter’s Hall” would have been, is [ wartime redacted – blacked out! ]

An article in Isle of Thanet Gazette 20th August 1910, reprinted in Monkton News. It shows a Garden Fete in grounds of Walters Hall, hosted by Viscount & Viscountess MOLESWORTH – for the proposed new Working Men’s Institute in Monkton.

During WW1, artillery soldiers with horses and guns were billeted at Walters Hall.

The son and heir of Viscount Molesworth, Lt The Hon Charles Willoughby Murray MOLESWORTH, of the 1st Battalion Duke of Cornwall’s Light Infantry, died of his wounds at the 11th Canadian Field Ambulance on 15th April 1917 aged 19. Born in Umballa, India, he lived at Walter’s Hall. He is interred at Villers Station cemetery, France. He is commemorated on the Westgate-on-Sea War Memorial, Kent.

In 1933 it was used as a depot by Callenders Cables who put up the original pylons carrying the electric cables across the marshes (for approx. 3 years).

In 1935 Mrs Elizabeth WETHERBY-WILLIAMS bought the grim looking Georgian mansion “Walters Hall”, a local builder did it up, and it was reinvented as “Walters Hall Country Club”, opening in 1938.

In WW2 it was also a military billet and well used by the military based at Manston Airport.

Mrs WETHERBY-WILLIAMS and her 2 grown up daughters had engaged “six beautiful young women as dancing partners”, and it became a lively entertainment hub, providing local employment and trade for taxi drivers and caterers and staff etc, but they suddenly disappeared when the lights went out in the house during a function, leaving many unpaid debts having “done a runner”! “She created the impression that she was a woman of considerable means and boasted that she had wealthy connections abroad. Now she has disappeared and her mansion of mystery is deserted.” There was a newspaper article about it at the time.

Upon becoming derelict it was demolished in 1948 and replaced by the present two bungalows on its imprint. Some villagers recall playing in the ruins.

Mrs WETHERBY-WILLIAMS's eldest daughter, Margaret (1901-1984) was born in Canada, and became a popular crime writer, ‘MARGARET ERSKINE’, with 20+ novels all featuring Inspector Septimus Finch. These novels all feature large, eccentric families, old houses, secret rooms, psychic phenomena and familiar puzzles. An Internet search reveals that a year after Elizabeth disappeared, she was in Detroit, Michigan with her son Arthur. He died there. Elizabeth was last traced to Bexhill-on-Sea in 1946.

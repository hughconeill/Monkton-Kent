## Right: “Methodist Chapel”

*Dates from c1874.*

The land for the building of the Chapel belonged to “Golden Point Farm” next door, possibly by St Dunstan’s Blind Association (owners of “Golden Point Farm”).

About 1971, a group of ladies from the village set up the “Monday Birds”, and they met at the Methodist Chapel once a month. It was an antidote to the men-only MENS INSTITUTE on the other side of the road. Some of the ladies involved were Julie COOK, Jackie PLUCKROSE, Marjorie CULLY, Beryl JAKEMAN, Jean FULLER, Nellie TAYLOR and Pat KIRKE.

Percy Edwin “Dido” SMITH (d. 1948) started a band at Monkton Methodist Chapel. He was also the bandmaster of the Minster Salvation Army Band. He lived in Sheriffs Court Lane.

A 1966 Thanet Gazette newspaper article about Monkton, noted that the Methodist Church Hall is the centre of weekly entertainment for the Methodists.

### The latest update – Monkton Chapel: a small place with a long history

*From its ancient links with Canterbury to a modern community campaign, this rural chapel continues to serve the village. By Lauraine Lee, Monkton Chapel Steward.*

In the quiet agricultural landscape of Monkton, near Ramsgate, stands a modest Chapel with a story far larger than its size. Surrounded by traditional Kentish farmland and cottages, Monkton Chapel has long been a place of worship, fellowship and local identity.

**A Chapel on Monkton Street.** Historic records date the present Chapel to 1874. It was built beside the dusty track that became Monkton Street, in a small rural settlement shaped by agriculture and village life.

During the late 1870s, the Reverend Ewan Maclachlan was among the first ministers to oversee Monkton together with Birchington and Acol as the area developed an independent district status. The Chapel became part of a wider pattern of worship across these neighbouring communities. For generations, this small rural Chapel has linked Monkton’s ancient past with the everyday life of its community.

**A new chapter in 2024.** After turbulent times and a period of disrepair, the future of Monkton Chapel became uncertain. In 2024, a group of like-minded local people came together to help save it from closure. Lauraine Lee, who lives next to the Chapel in Monkton Cottages, approached the Southeast Methodist Circuit with a plan. The aim was not only to raise funds for the building, but also to create outreach groups that would benefit the local community. The vision placed wellbeing at the heart of the Chapel’s future: supporting mental health, reducing isolation and offering people a welcoming place where they could feel that they belonged.

**Music, movement and friendship.** Lauraine began “Singing for Joy”, a regular group welcoming people to sing music of all kinds. The group has donated all its proceeds towards the Chapel’s planned refurbishment, combining friendship and enjoyment with practical support for the building’s future. After two successful years, Lauraine also established “Social Shuffle”, a meditation and dance group designed to help its members stay well, socially connected and physically active. Wendy and Helen now host popular monthly “Art Classes” at the chapel, with sessions regularly full and a growing waiting list. Their welcoming approach encourages budding creatives to build confidence, try new techniques and explore a variety of artistic styles in a friendly, supportive setting. The Chapel is also preparing to launch “Mankind”, a new men’s group centred on wellbeing, connection and friendship. The groups aim will offer a welcoming, informal space to talk, build friendships and make group plans for days out or supporting the community or just sit and chat. It is another important step in the chapel’s mission to support the wellbeing of the whole community.

**A growing team.** Today, eight members help to oversee the chapel alongside its new deacon, Laura McBean, and her husband, the Reverend Duncan McBean. Their passion, drive and wealth of experience have brought a renewed sense of Methodist service to Monkton Chapel. Laura and Duncan are now a valued and regular part of the chapel’s outreach and worship, offering wholehearted support as it grows into a welcoming community space with faith firmly at its heart.

**Longstanding friendship and service.** Among the Chapel’s most enduring supporters is Delphine Mitchell, its longest-serving parishioner. Together with her husband, Peter Mitchell, she has been a true mainstay and friend of Monkton Chapel. Delphine continues to attend worship regularly and generously provides refreshments for the Chapel’s outreach activities, while Maggie another lovely member also gives valued support to the Chapel community. Music has always brought warmth and joy to Monkton Chapel, and Stuart Horsburgh plays a special part in that tradition. A longstanding member, much-valued friend and gifted pianist, Stuart regularly fills the Chapel with music at services and community events. Encouraging musical creativity remains an important part of our mission, and we are now seeking funding for a new piano—an instrument that will support Stuart’s playing and help music flourish in the Chapel for years to come. Rachel Horsburgh and David Blackwell serve as Chapel readers, helping to lead worship, while Ann Andrews is the Chapel’s safeguarding officer. Together, the members contribute distinct skills and responsibilities to the life, care and future of Monkton Chapel.

**Leadership, structure and support.** Dr Peggy Kabonde, Superintendent of the circuit, has had a major impact on Monkton Chapel. Her guidance and understanding have helped steer the Chapel towards growth and development. With her support, clear roles and responsibilities have now been established for each member of the team. Behind the Chapel’s remarkable progress is a dedicated team working with energy and purpose. As treasurer, Barry Lee carefully manages the finances, helps keep the Chapel viable and oversees the refurbishment project and property. Lauraine Lee has designed the new space, drawing on her background in interior design to create a flexible, welcoming setting for many different uses. Sara Archer, the local parish clerk and Chapel secretary, has brought invaluable skill and determination to the fundraising effort, successfully securing grants for the refurbishment and community plans. So much has been achieved in such a short time through hard work, excellent communication and a shared passion for the mission. Together with donations from the Chapel’s outreach activities, an impressive £73,000 has been raised in just one year—and fundraising continues. With funding now secured, the complete refurbishment of Monkton Chapel is planned for completion in summer 2027. Monkton Chapel still stands amid the Kentish countryside, and services are held every two weeks. Alongside worship, its growing programme of music, movement and fellowship, creative arts reflect the determination of local people to protect both a historic building and the community life it represents. From its association with Christ Church Priory to the fully funded refurbishment planned for completion in summer 2027, the Chapel’s story is one of continuity, care and renewal. It remains a quiet but important landmark—a place of worship and belonging where Monkton’s history and its hopes for the future meet. If you would like to become part of Monkton Chapel’s incredible journey of hope, please come along and join the team. This is only the beginning!

With every blessing - Lauraine Lee. (2026)

## Right: “Golden Point Farm” (also “Vulcan Villa”)

It was a Market Garden small holding of 30 acres farmed by Clarence ‘Clary’ DAWS. “Golden Point Farm” was owned by St Dunstan’s Blind Association, a charity for WW1 blind veterans. Mr PINK was just such a blind veteran and he was a familiar figure in the village selling eggs from his chicken farm. The small holding had chickens, pigs and also arable crops. Clary was a valuable source of early employment for the youngsters of the village, getting their first taste of work at an early age, and having some cash to spend on treats. There used to be a well in the back garden, used by several of the villagers, but its location is not known.

## Left: #1, #2, #3 “Delce Cottages” and #4 “The Old Post Office”

*Dates from 1766 – 1799. Grade II listed in 1986.*

\#4 “Delce Cottages” dates from 1739 and was an early official Post Office for Monkton. Photos of “The Old Post Office” show a rare post box. Prior to 1895 letter boxes were made and installed at the expense of the local sub postmaster and until the 1880’s was not of a standard type. The postmaster would have engaged a local craftsman to manufacture a suitable box, and the Monkton box is a rare survivor of this type of box. They are sometimes called ‘carpenter’s boxes’.

This rare post box is at the “Old Post Office”. The building itself was Grade II listed in 1986 and dates from the mid 1700’s. Set into the front wall is a wooden post box bearing an unusual, enamelled sign :- “LETTERS which contain coins will, if posted as ordinary letters, be charged on delivery with a SPECIAL REGISTRATION FEE of EIGHTPENCE”. Money letters were first introduced in 1792 as a means of combatting the theft of coins in letters. This registration was the forerunner of today’s registered letter system.”

(The Letter Box Study Group – there’s a Society for everything, somewhere!).

By the 1850’s, after Rowland Hill’s Penny Postage Bill (1839), the postal service was universal across all villages.

When the Village Post Office and Store closed in the 1990’s, Bernard COLE set up a small Post Office operation in the grounds of his building business next to the original C18th site.

## Left: “Monkton Village Hall”

A WW1 ex- Army Nissan hut from Richborough Camp from 1923, donated to the Women’s Institute, under the leadership of Mrs E GORE (the memorial in the front wall is “the Gore Clock” and paid for by public collection and placed in position after her death) and erected in 1926. It was a rare double-faced clock, but because of timing issues and that someone had to wind it up every week, a similar (the present) double-faced clock replaced it.

Monkton WI was set up in 1988 in Monkton, and away from the Minster organisation. Edith ‘Nellie’ WRAIGHT, Delphine MITCHELL, and Mavis JONES were early organisers of the move. The organisation is still a popular activity for the village today, with a full programme of events and activities.

Wilfred Fitzmaurice MORRIS (b1872) retired doctor, living at “The Croft”, raised money for the Village Hall. He used to make trays, baskets, and rugs.

Julie COOK and Pat NEATE started a Youth Club in the Village Hall every Friday evening. A Christmas Panto written by them. There was football on the School field with Ken KING and Peter ATTWELL. Bernard COLE’s coach was used for outings to Minnis Bay. Terry MARSH ran a Youth Club at the Men’s Institute. Dennis and Ann ANDREWS also formed a Youth Club at the Hall in the 2000’s. *Apologies to anyone I’ve missed out whose contribution should be noted!*

Having very limited facilities themselves, Monkton School have used the Village Hall for their PE lessons, assemblies, music events, plays, and for their lunchtime meals. The extension and modernisation of the School in the 2007 made this unnecessary.

An old Conveyance Certificate dated 1922 from Mr H.T. Willett of Parsonage Farm to Mr A.W. Miller of Monkton Rd Farm AND Others – sold for £4 11s 4d (=£4.56p) – a piece of land SW of the public highway leading from Canterbury to Minster, and bought the year before from the Ecclesiastical Commissioners for England, forming part of the Drove Field. A ‘Prohibition’ notice says: “No building or any other erection shall be erected on the paid piece or parcel of land EXCEPT A VILLAGE HALL, and no such building erected thereon shall at any time be used except for the purposes of ENTERTAINMENTS, DANCES, CONCERTS and other events usually held in a Village Hall for the benefit and enjoyment of the residents in the village of Monkton”. ## Left: “Men’s Institute”

In the early 1900’s, to raise funds for the Men’s Institute, a posh garden party was suggested. It was held in the grounds of “Monkton Hall”, owned by Viscount MOLESWORTH, and their committee of ladies consisted of Viscountess MOLESWORTH, Mrs PATRY (ref. “Patry’s Cottages” on the site of “Barnside”???), Mrs Val SMITH, Mrs W.W. WILLIAMS (wife of the vicar), Mrs F de B COLLARD, Mrs H.T. WILLETT and Mrs BEVAN. There were live pets, a rifle range, a conjuror, refreshments, illuminated Chinese lanterns in the evening and music from the Buffs Army Band.

1911 census - the ORIGINAL building was not open yet.

1950’s W.S. COLE built a new Men’s Institute, to replace the original one (where #116“Wilburnham” is now, using the same Men’s Institute front wall).

Men’s Institute was tea-total, with games like darts, and female free.

The Monkton Village Youth Club was based there, organised by Terry MARSH.

In the mid 1970’s, it was used by the Monkton Playgroup Under 5’s, registered for 10 children, resulting from a chat with Jane STUPPLES (who worked at Spurgeon Homes), and Denyse COLE, who was able to offer use of the Men’s Institute as a base. Volunteers such as Rosemary HUNT, Pat KIRKE, Priscilla MARSH, and Mrs Elsie ATTWELL cleaned it and searched for free toys and games while a committee was formed, and it opened for 3 mornings a week. Bernard Cole lit the fire every morning for them throughout the winter. Through various grants they were able to increase the contact time up to 5 mornings per week which was more helpful to working mums. The leaders over these early years were Priscilla RENNISON, Evelyn LAWRENCE nee Morris, Heather GUNTON and Judy BERRYMAN. Feeding the lambs in a field on the road to St Nicholas at Wade was a popular annual activity with the children.

Another initiative for the Men’s Institute was the “Mother & Toddler” group every Wednesday afternoon led by Heather GUNTON & Kim GILLIES.

*Apologies for any other helpers who started this invaluable daily provision for Monkton village families yet are unnamed here.*

School concerts were held in the School before the Village Hall was built in 1926.

Village Concerts in the 1920’s were held in the School to raise money for the Village Hall. Families used to take extra chairs to sit on.

Headmistress Mrs SIMPSON would teach sword dancing to pupils in the 1920’s.

The portion of ground where the mobile classrooms used to be, behind the school, used to be vegetable allotments, and the boys would work in 2s & 3s per plot – good training for later life.

The vicar would call into the School most mornings.

At the special Christmas dinner, Michael SMITH from “Monkton Court Farm” supplied the turkey and fresh vegetables. All the other goodies for Christmas – fruit, sweets, and a toy each – were supplied by Major HEDLEY of “New House” (“Field House”).

In the 1950’s there was no lollipop lady at the School, so the bus driver (a normal service bus, not a school special) would always get out and escort children across the road and into the playground.

Monkton School belonged to “Wilfred’s League of Gugnuncs”, sponsored by The Daily Mirror. It was a club from 1927 linked to the strip cartoon, particularly popular in the southeast seaside towns. Fans of “Pip, Squeak & Wilfred” had a coded message :- “Ick ick par boo?” and members replied, “Goo goo par nunk”!

In the late 1970’s / early ‘80’s, there was an outbreak of TB in the school - the Headmistress and 2 pupils, who were sent to the Sea Bathing Hospital in Margate to recover. Everyone in the School had to be BCG vaccinated.

Headmaster Sam SEXTON was retiring at Easter and only a 12-month temporary head would be appointed. In the 1987 hurricane, Friday 16th October, the school wall fell down, but School governor Bernard COLE was quick to rebuild it so that classes could resume asap. Here are Headteacher Mrs Janet Graham’s notes on the event as it affected them.

> “Hurricane force winds struck the south of the county in the early hours of Friday morning and caused extensive damage to buildings and trees. A local radio broadcast at 7am informed us that all Kent schools were to be closed for the day and to venture outside was extremely dangerous. There was no public transport at all in Kent – no trains or buses due to thousands of trees down across roads and railway lines. Much of southern England lost its electricity supply for days – some for as much as 2 weeks! A cross-Channel ferry was blown onto rocks outside Folkestone. Kew Gardens lost 1,000 out of 11,000 trees, including many old and famous specimens. Sevenoaks lost 6 of its famous 7 oak trees.
>

>
> A school fair organised by the PTFA in Minster went ahead the next day, Saturday 17th. This was a difficult day as most people were clearing up after the storm and were without electricity. There was also an air of ‘shell-shock’ with people trying to come to terms with a changed landscape and the buffeting they had received. Nevertheless £100 was raised for PTFA funds.
>
> On Monday 19th October, most Kent schools opened, but Monkton remained closed to children as there is still structural damage to be repaired, the power cables to be reconnected and the playground cleared of rubbish. All the staff reported for work and cleared and cleaned books and equipment.

## #2 “Parsonage Cottages” (“Waggon Cottage”)

*Dates from 1740.*

Monkton villager Charlie WOODCOCK (1910 – 1985, buried in Monkton Churchyard) was a waggoner and wheelwright and lived here in the 1960’s before moving to a bungalow he had built on the corner of soon-to-be-built Seamark Close. He was a leading charitable light in village activities and was instrumental in getting the Sports Field and Sports Pavilion created for village use. A memorial bench has recently been placed in the corner of the Sports Field, by the Beacon. He was one of the ‘characters’ of the village where he spent his whole life.

The cottage was able to reclaim bricks for an extension when the “Docker Hill” shepherds hut on the marshes was demolished.

In the late ‘60’s, Bernard COLE made 2 cottages into 1 for Londoner Mr ELSTROM. He drove an Aston Martin DB4 and owned a dress factory, and the Village Hall hosted a high couture fashion sale. Later his MK2 Jaguar got stuck in a snowdrift on Willett’s Hill – 1963 in the Big Freeze? When villagers started digging it out, they realised they were digging out another car that was stuck in front of it!

Parish Council chairman Robin TAPSALL (b1945) also lived here. Between 1961 – 62 he was a wrestler, known as “Young Robby”.

Renowned British artist Gilbert RUMBOLD (1896 – 1974) has made drawings of this traditional old cottage – in a style that he has used in many other paintings of old village buildings.

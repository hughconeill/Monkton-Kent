# The Centenary Mile Walk of 1926

**Monkton remembered in 2026 – what Monkton looked like 100 years ago**

Change is all around us. I hope it’s possible to strip back the many recent
changes in our village of Monkton (or Monocstun – the “Monks’ Land”) to reveal
the fertile farming community of 100 years ago.

My “Centenary Mile Walk of 1926” will take you along that journey pointing out
the small tied farm cottages, the large houses, and the larger farmhouses.
Amongst and between them are the barns and sheds to store the harvest of hops,
cherries, strawberries, apples, cucumbers, cauliflowers, arable crops and
grazing land for sheep, cows, pigs, horses, turkeys and chickens. As well as
the big farms, there are the Market Garden small holdings that were an
important ingredient in feeding the village and were sold to other local
markets. If you’re looking at a house, and it’s not on the 1926 map, then it
used to be a hedge, bush, tree, arable field and grazing land, a fence, barn
or shed!

**Try as I might to be accurate, I’m no expert, so I apologise for any errors
and omissions, and welcome your personal knowledge to correct me. I’m always
happy to talk about this project.**

The “Centenary Mile Walk” is only Stage 1 of this project. I’m still
collecting more stories from Monkton villagers about the life they lived here,
and I hope it will be a fascinating insight for everyone, and a resource for
future generations. Thank you.

*Stuart Horsburgh*

## The world in 1926

1926! What was going on in the world, post-World War 1?

- Old Age Pensions introduced a contributary state scheme of 10s (50p) a week from the age of 65.
- BBC radio was in its 4th year of broadcasting.
- John Logie Baird demonstrates his ‘mechanical television’, 10 years before the first BBC TV broadcast.
- The famous red telephone box was introduced chiefly in London and then extended nationwide.
- The 9-day General Strike against reduced wages and poor working conditions, brought about a state of emergency, and Britain came to a standstill.
- CPRE, the Campaign for the Preservation of Rural England, is founded, to limit urban sprawl and protect the countryside.
- A.A. Milne’s children’s book “Winnie-the-Pooh” is published.
- Queen Elizabeth II was born to King George VI, she lived to be 96 years old, and ruled for 70 years, the longest reigning British Monarch.
- The first transatlantic phone call, from London to New York, is completed.
- Britain’s first traffic lights come into use, in London’s Piccadilly Circus.
- Famous escapologist and illusionist Harry Houdini dies, after being punched unexpectedly in the stomach.
- The era of silent films ended when “The Jazz Singer” was released in Hollywood the following year.
- The “Roaring Twenties” featured cars, jazz music, flappers, dancing and art deco.
- Labour’s Ramsey MacDonald and Conservative Stanley Baldwin were alternating Prime Ministers from 1923 to 1937.
- Since the Model T Ford rolled off the production line in 1908, car production in GB was dominated by the Morris Cowley, and Austin Seven, with car ownership just starting to expand beyond the wealthy elite.

If the free Monkton News was being delivered, there would be just about 100
households for a population of about 420 people.

Old villagers born in the 1920’s and interviewed in the 1980’s said:
“Everybody knew everybody else, from one end of the village to the other.”

The principal farms in Monkton were Sheriffs Court Farm, Fuller’s Farm,
Vinson’s Farm, Baxter’s Farm, Parsonage Farm and Monkton Court Farm. Market
Garden small holdings were worked by Peter Crook, Doug Stannard and Clary
Daws.

The commercial businesses in the village were the “New Inn” pub; the “Shop &
Post Office” (next to the pub); the cobbler Fred Rolfe; Fred Harrison the
blacksmith (the smithy was in the pub car park next to the Post Office);
George Gore the carpenter / coffin maker; W.S. Coles the builders; the
Methodist Chapel; the Village School; the Village Hall; the Men’s Institute;
and St Mary Magdalene C of E Church.

It’s possible to imagine Monkton Street without the ‘recent’ new building
developments of Acreage Way; Monocstune Mews; John Douglas Drive; Collards
Close; Seamark Close; Vicarage Gardens; Parsonage Fields; Old Orchard Drive;
and Monkton Court – of the past 100 years. All of those (except Monocstune
Mews – it replaced Drake & Fletchers engineering business), replaced
farmland, orchards, arable crops, grazing land, market gardens, barns and farm
buildings.

And then if you take away all the post WW2 buildings, and various extensions
and porches, you are left with the original cosy farm-workers cottages,
senior farm worker houses, and the big farmhouses.

In the gaps were hedges, trees, fields and barns. Monkton Street as seen in
the old postcards and photos would not be tarmacked; no pavements; no street
lighting; transport by horse and cart because there were very few cars.

Now let’s take the “Centenary Mile Walk from 1926” along the B2047 (Monkton
Street, sometimes just called “The Street”), from Sheriffs Court Lane to
Monkton Church, to appreciate the type of world that earlier generations
experienced. To begin this walk of 1926 Monkton, start at Sheriffs Court
Lane, looking LEFT and RIGHT as directed.

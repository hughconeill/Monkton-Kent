# Special Stories

*Stuart Horsburgh’s favourite stories, snippets and curiosities gathered while
researching the [Centenary Mile Walk](centenary-walk/index.md).*

## Nuclear war!

My favourite story has to be Eric BLADES, owner of the “Village Shop & Post Office” in the 1980’s, given the job of ringing the “3 Minute Bell” to warn the village in the event of a nuclear attack! I hope the residents at either end of Monkton Street had good hearing! I wonder whose job it is now? Monkton’s bellringers could ring a peal of ‘Plain Hunt’ if necessary!

## Bird scaring!

My second favourite story comes from George NORMAN, who speaking in 1991 said: “When I was still at school, during the holidays, I would do bird scaring from 5am to 7pm, 7 days per week, for £1.50s a week.” I presume it involved running around the orchards, waving and shouting at the birds who are eating the ripening produce. Probably more effective than a scarecrow and quieter than a bird-scarer machine. But can you imagine children of today opting for doing that in their school holidays? No time for shopping at Westwood Cross, or jetting off to Disneyworld.

## Closed!

The top road closure in 1989 – 3 drivers were killed in a year, and following a police points system it was closed. It was always a dangerous turn onto the main A299 road. The keys to the barrier are with the Fire Brigade and funeral director Jonathan COLE for emergencies. Once there was an accident at the Monkton roundabout when a tanker crashed and emitted a gas cloud. It caused chaos and gridlock in the village as lorry satnavs rerouted them up to the Church. Although initially reluctant to open the gate, the police eventually gave permission, and traffic was allowed to escape onto the Canterbury Road at Gore St.

## Tunnels!

Some people have mentioned the existence of tunnels in the village? I won’t speculate upon their purpose or where they are, but it’s to be expected of an ancient village such as ours.

## Ghosts!

And that goes for ghosts too! Haunted houses, and ghosts in quite ordinary houses are certainly real for the people who have seen them. They are all friendly as far as I’ve been told!

## Onions!

A flood in 1973 caused chaos at the Willetts Hill end of the village. Onions, which had been planted the wrong way, tumbled down the field and into the back gardens. There was an up side to this disaster of course! Householders didn’t need to buy onions for quite a long time afterwards!

## “Tom from the desert”!

Another heart-warming story is, as I understand it, the one of Tom CULLY, from Minster. He was in the Hampshire Reg., stationed nearby at “Walter’s Hall” (now demolished), and met Marjorie FULLER at the Methodist Chapel. He wrote her many letters whilst away in North Africa during WW2. When he returned, he knocked on her door, her father said, “Who are you?” And he replied, “I’m Tom from the desert!” - coming home to marry his sweetheart. And they lived happily ever after.

## Father Christmas!

In recent times, Santa has installed himself in the Village Hall to welcome children (young and old) on Christmas Eve. But there was a time, certainly in the 1980’s when I experienced it, when Santa and his Snowman pulled their sleigh of carefully addressed presents from one end of the village to the other on Christmas Day. I remember getting an orange if I promised to do the washing up after the Christmas dinner. Obviously the village has been grateful to those ‘Santas’ over the years for the delight and pleasure we have all received from their charitable actions!

## Monkton Street addresses!

I think it is charming that all the post would be delivered to cottage addresses, rather than numbers. I’m thinking it could have been in the 1970’s when the street numbering system was modernised. But long-time Monkton residents still remember their old ‘cottage address’.

## Roads!

The Monkton roads were very rough, all stones, and it was a horse and trap that would be used for transport. The road was tarmacked only for the lower (Minster) end of the village. The rest of the road, up to the “Royal Exchange” and main road was always stoney. Monkton Street was called the ‘summer’ road. And the top road (A299) was the ‘winter’ road.

## Doctor!

It was remembered that Dr. HUNT attended his village patients on a bike, wearing a bowler hat, and charged 2/6d (12.5p today) to visit you. Someone else remembered it as 3/6d (17.5p today) per visit. Expensive in those days so many people just had to “grin and bear it” or resort to home remedies.

## Health & safety!

I liked the idea of ‘Musketry’ being taught to pupils in 1914; and ‘Sword dancing’ being taught in the 1920’s, at Monkton School. In the 1990’s, Seamark Close resident, Don GREGG, got close to this with his dramatic reconstructions in the classroom of a volcanic eruption for Monkton schoolchildren! Never one to do things by halves was Don! I was surprised to read that he produced in traditional copperplate script, the ornate list of Monkton Vicars since 1291 that is in the porchway of the Church.

## Long days!

I’m humbled to hear that youngsters went to work when they were only 14 years old, and worked from 6am to 5pm, and to 1pm on a Saturday on the farms. Thank goodness for Sundays (unless you had to go to 4 Sunday church services !)

## Dance marathon!

I’ve been told that the young girls/ladies of the village would attend the Saturday Night Dances……IN WICKHAMBREUX! 7.8 miles away! They would walk over the marshes and then call in for a cup of tea nightcap with the Docker Hill shepherds hut family on the way back. Amazing because I’d think twice about doing that in a car!

## Traders!

It’s remembered that a familiar part of daily village life would be the visits ( and cries? ) from the Rag n Bone Man, the Shrimp Man, and the Milkman. There were grocers who delivered each week e.g. Roses from Sandwich, C. Butcher & Sons from Ramsgate, Packhams from St Nicholas at Wade and Woods (Rooks) from Ramsgate. There were several butchers that made deliveries. Mrs VINSON used to have a small farm shop. There was a regular carrier service, usually on Tuesdays, from Ramsgate to Canterbury and return. They would collect and drop off (Baldwin, Hughes & Drake, Ramsgate.) So present-day Amazon vans and Tesco deliveries are not new!

## Ship ahoy!

People often ask about the “SEAMARK”. What was it? Where was it? The Seamark ROAD! obelisk, about 30 feet high, on the brow of the hill on Seamark Road (but now covered over by Thanet Earth) was built in1791 by Trinity House to replace a windmill (taken down in about 1782, but it ended up in nearby Sarre as the predecessor of the present mill) which had stood on the spot for many years, as a marine navigation aid, of brick construction. This was demolished about 1922, and Charles Igglesden wrote in 1932 that the inscribed foundation stone can still be seen in the rock garden of Monkton Court.

## Deported!

NEWSPAPER REPORT – “Landowners Greed Sparked Rural Revolt.” 1830 ‘Swing Riots’ started at Lower Hardres in Kent, wanting higher wages and an end to the threshing machine. Troops were sent in to end the riots. 19 were sentenced to death; 738 transported to Tasmania/Australia; 644 imprisoned; 7 fined; 1 whipped; 800 acquitted or bound over to keep the peace.”

Among the convicted rioters deported to Van Diemen’s Land (Tasmania) from MONKTON were :- Stephen Bushell (b.1802); William Bushell (b.1812); Elizabeth Studman (b.1810). So, it’s probable that some present-day Australians have their family roots in Monkton.

## Bonjour mes amis!

It was mooted in the 1994 Parish Centenary Celebrations Booklet that a door-to-door canvas be undertaken to see if Monkton wanted to start a Twinning Association with the French town of O’oost Cappel, 20 minutes from Dunkirk, and close to the Belgian border. It obviously never happened!

## Village crest!

This crest was designed for the village celebrations in 1953 for the coronation of Queen Elizabeth II, by W.S. COLE and A.J. GRAY. It shows the 4 historic features in the village. A – its punishment (the stocks); B – its early residents (a monk); C – the main crop (hops); and D – the Seamark to guide the sailors (the Seamark obelisk). And a midpoint wavy line to depict the sea when the village had its own sea wall !

## Monkton Nature Reserve!

The 16 acre chalk pit began life in 1799 when chalk was excavated for the construction of Ramsgate harbour. Small scale digging continued for nearly 150 years until 1942 when there was a large excavation to help with the forming of a longer aircraft runway at Manston. More chalk was removed in 1953 when the sea wall broke close to Reculver and a large inner basin was formed (whilst the sea defences were repaired) with Monkton chalk and weatherproofed with Whitstable clay. In 1958 the last large excavation took place to help in the construction of the Richborough Power Station. It includes the UK’s 1st artificial bat cave, constructed in 1986 as a hibernaculum to support local bat populations.

## Grass track racing!

In the 1960’s, on the corner of the road in Gore Street, leading down to Plucks Gutter, there was Grass Track Motor Bike Racing.

## It’s a record!

Xmas 1957 - A Thanet cow has set up a new national record for a one day’s milk yield for Guernseys. It’s “Gore St Top”, owned by Trevor WILLETT of Gore St Farm, Monkton, which in two machine milkings in 24 hours gave 103 pints – just over 10 gallons!

## That’s telling them!

In a 1972 Monthly Church Magazine for Minster & Monkton, the vicar of Monkton, Revd GOWER-SMITH, writes in his Letter :- “I cannot let the opportunity go by without writing a word of great disappointment and sorrow that the attendances for the Whitsunday were so small. I have never in all my ministry had such a miserable Sunday regarding the congregations for this great Festival of the Church, and I sincerely hope that I may never experience such indifference from our people to so great a DAY. The choir turned up magnificently but the general congregation ?. ?. ?. We will leave it at that.”

## To be or not to be!

On Thursday 25th January 1990 (and Friday 26th), the “Monkton Amateur Dramatics” (M.A.D.) presented 3 one-act plays in the Village Hall. “Madhatters in Mayfair” by Barbara van Klampen; “Cat’s Paw” by Paul Reakes; and “False Pretences”, again by Paul Reakes. Their next production would be “Goose Chase” – at a date in the future. Produced & Directed by Eric BLADES (P.O. & Shop) and Daphne GREGG (Seamark Close, and the Gore St farm shop on the road to St Nicholas at Wade – where the “Cave Fencing” business is now).

## Open Gardens dance weekend!

Newspaper article – 2000 : “Villagers step in to rescue fund-raiser”. Instead of the bi-annual Open Gardens Weekend, the committee organised a dance weekend that raised £800. Local ceilidh band “Contraband” hosted a barn dance and the “Line Rangers” held a line-dancing workshop. The “White Stag” was turned into a disco and enthusiasts had a go at Scottish dancing. Everyone enjoyed themselves and “it was a hoot!” The Monkton Open Gardens Weekend was started in 1992 with the money raised going to the Church and the Village Hall. In an Isle of Thanet Gazette article 19.6.1992, 4 photos show work being done in their gardens prior to the Weekend – featuring Davina RANSOM, Tom CULLY, Brenda GRAHAM and Gilly BROWN. Monkton Open Gardens Weekend continues to be a fun and successful village event every 2 years.

As well as having actress/comedian Miriam MARGOYLES living in the village, Great British Bake Off judge Paul HOLLYWOOD also lived in the village while he had a baker’s shop in Canterbury. His child was christened in Monkton Church. And I wouldn’t be surprised if one of our previous Parish Council chairmen, Robin Tapsell, was to be seen on Saturday afternoon TV in the early 1960’s, wrestling as “YOUNG ROBBY”, against the likes of Mick McManus and “blond Adonis Shirley Crabtree” (aka Big Daddy)!

## Spag bol-gate!

And talking of Paul Hollywood.......?  It was an innocuous boast by Chris Penney in the "White Stag" pub that he made the BEST bolognese in the village and this couldn’t be left unchallenged.  A number of the regulars were invited to join in a formal competition and Chris, Brian Ward, Hugh O’Neill, another guy(Lynns friend - trying to get a name), John and Tommy (John’s son, the chef at The Stag) took up the challenge.  The competition was only open to men and took place on the 8th Oct 2004.  John Esqulant, the landlord, knew Paul Hollywood from when he lived in the village and asked him to judge the competition.  Paul had a regular show at that time on one of the satellite channels so was a well-known chef and a minor celebrity. 

The entries were numbered randomly and Margaret (John’s wife) heated each in the microwave and gave them to Paul to taste not knowing whose was whose. ## Band music!

It’s good to know that a Band in Monkton, at the Methodist Chapel, was started by Percy Edwin SMITH (known as ‘Dido’ Smith), when he was also bandmaster of the Minster Salvation Army Band as well. I’m happy to also mention “CONTRABAND / SCOTCH MIST” as a Monkton-based folk group for ceilidhs, barn dances, Scottish 25th January Burns night events that has existed for many years and performed in the “Village Hall”. Also the “MONKTON VILLAGE BAND” that was formed in the 1990’s resulting from an open “invitation to play” advert in the Monkton News, with John (gt), Heidi (voc), Neil (bass) and Stuart (keys/sax). The Monkton News of July 2008 shows a photo of a later version of the band, “KNIGHTS OF THE ROAD”. In recent years, from 2013, the village has welcomed fund-raising evenings and private celebrations by “DNA”, a versatile rock band performing great songs from the 1950’s to the present day (although not much from the present day!) The line up has been Chris (voc/gt), Neil (voc/bass), Stuart (keys/sax) and pop queen Suzi Quatro’s drummer Dave Neal – replaced by Andy when Dave died in 2020. Stand out moments have been a whole evening of Beatles songs to commemorate the 60th anniversary in March 2023 of ‘Beatlemania’. And the most recent special evening in May 2026 of “Rock & Roll Hits from 1954-1964”. Another regular music event are fund-raising singalong concerts by “Knight & Daze”, the duo of Stuart (keys) and Adrian (gt/banjo), performing popular ‘olde tyme songs’ of the 20th century (and sometimes older!)

## Carol singing!

Relatives of the FULLER’S from Manston would load a piano onto a trailer and sing Christmas carols at the homes of the generous, friendly farmers in Monkton and Gore Street. In the 1980’s the Minster Salvation Army Band would go to various village locations and also play outside the pub. As would Betteshanger Brass Band, and also the ‘Horsburgh Family Monkton Ensemble’ (+friends) – every Christmas Eve outside the “White Stag” pub in all weathers by the Christmas tree, followed by mulled wine and mince pies. In recent years this has moved to the Christmas tree outside the “Village Hall” when Santa has finished giving his presents out on Christmas Eve. Keeping the old traditions alive isn’t such a bad thing.

## A hundred years old!

We have in the Monkton churchyard, the grave ([#165](../../church/churchyard/numbered-stones.md), on the South side of the church) of Hilda Lily SALT, born in 1889, “who departed this life 21st December 1989, aged 100 YEARS” ! So she lived through such momentous events as :- the 2 Boer Wars (1880 – 1902); the death of Queen Victoria in 1901; the Wright Brothers “Flyer 1” took flight in 1903; the first Model T Ford car in 1908; World War 1 (1914-18); Captain Scott just loses to Norwegian Roald Amundsen to reach the South Pole and dies on his return in 1912; the Titanic hits an iceberg on its maiden voyage and sinks in 1912; the Russian Revolution in 1917; Howard Carter discovers the Tomb of Tutankhamun in 1922; the first televisual image by Scotsman John Logie Baird in 1925; the General Strike of 8 days in 1926 that brought the country to a standstill; “the Jazz Singer” starring Al Jolson was the first ‘talkie’ at the cinema in 1927;the year 1928 saw the accidental rediscovery of penicillin by Scotsman Alexander Fleming, the first appearance of Micky Mouse in “Steamboat Willie”, and the invention of bubble gum; the American Wall Street Crash in 1929 led to the worldwide Great Depression; the first high definition tv broadcast by the BBC was in 1936; “Snow White & the Seven Dwarfs” is the first animated feature film from Disney in 1937; World War 2 (1939-45); in 1953 British climber Edmund Hillary reached the top of Mt Everest, with the coronation of Queen Elizabeth II 4 days later; the Russians launch Sputnik to begin the Space Race against America; Alaska and Hawaii joined the other 48 American states in 1959; the year 1963 gave us the Liverpool pop group The Beatles with their first LP hit “Please Please Me”, and the assassination of US President J. F. Kennedy in Dallas; the Troubles conflict in Northern Ireland started in 1968 until 1998; America’s Apollo 11 landed on the moon in 1969 and 2 men, Neil Armstrong & Buzz Aldrin, were the first humans to walk on its surface; the commercial release of Atari’s “Pong” was the launch of the video game industry in 1972; in 1978 Louise Joy Brown was the first baby born after IVF treatment; Margaret Thatcher becomes Britain’s first female Prime Minister in 1979; Britain went to war with Argentina about the sovereignty of the Falkland Islands, and Sony produced the 1st music CD, in 1982; the Great Storm of 1987 caused widespread hurricane force damage throughout Britain, unfortunately it was the day before the American Stock Market Crash of 1987 that reverberated around the world; 1988 was the start of the Channel Tunnel project with France; and finally, in 1989, Hilda SALT was hopefully able to witness the fall of the Berlin Wall, the fall of communism, the collapse of the Soviet Bloc, and the end of the Cold War! One more year and she’d have seen the start of the “World Wide Web” by Tim Berners-Lee, when our lives were all changed forever. She had a lot to reflect upon in her lifetime.

## One of the oldest!

From amongst the 257 graves that are in the churchyard, the oldest readable one that I have been able to decipher is that of Valentine CANTIS ([#149](../../church/churchyard/numbered-stones.md), on the East side of the church), a church warden, who died on 29th March 1732. The stone has weathered well for 294 years, protected by the East wall.

## But even older!

Inside the church, there is a [wall plaque](../../church/memorials-inside.md) even older! (#12 on the South wall at the back of the nave.) The second husband of Frances BLECHYNDEN (“she enjoyed 3 husbands”), was Nicholas ROBINSON, a gent of this parish, who died in 1594 – 431 years ago (she died on Christmas Day in 1611). So putting the year 1594 into context – Shakespeare’s play “A Comedy of Errors” was first performed, Queen Elizabeth I glowed in the recent success of routing the Spanish Armada, and England would soon establish its first successful colony in America, at Jamestown, Virginia.

A very contemporary mystery surrounds the ROBINSON name in Monkton, as we are trying to trace the son of Frances & Nicholas. Henry ROBINSON, whose will in 1642 left a lot of land locally in trust to his old college, St Johns College, Cambridge to provide bursaries for Monkton scholars and charity for poor villagers. St Johns College never fulfilled Henry’s wish during those 384 years! There is presently a judicial review to stop the College selling off the land in Birchington they don’t own for a huge housing development! Nowhere can we find where such a rich, single, Monkton man is buried, although we do have in the church the first part of the will written on a huge wooden board!

## The mystery of ‘Blue Dick’!

[RICHARD CULMER](../../church/richard-culmer.md) (1597-1662) is the unlisted vicar of Minster, who is buried in Monkton churchyard, but his whereabouts and remains are still a mystery!!! This infamous Puritan minister during the time of Cromwell, (known as the Commonwealth Period), was known locally as “Blue Dick” for wearing a blue cassock instead of the traditional black – because he thought he looked better in blue! From 1643 he was given the job of ‘commissioner’ at Canterbury Cathedral and he was overzealous in “the utter demolishing…of all monuments of superstition” which included priceless stained glass, sculptures, paintings and exterior adornments in the Cathedral and his other parishes of Goodnestone, Chartham, Harbledown, St Stephens Hackington and finally Minster. The Minster congregation finally threw him out, and him, his wife Katherine, and their 7 children all came to live in Monkton, where he died on 20th March 1662 – having been taken in by his neighbour the vicar of Monkton, Nicholas Thorowgood, of Parsonage House. His burial 2 days later in the Monkton churchyard is in an unknown spot and unmarked. Fitting, perhaps, for a man who managed to attract such universal anger and condemnation during his life.

## The original Old Post Office!

Monkton had a Post Office BEFORE the “Village Shop & Post Office” many of us remember next to the “White Stag” pub. It was at #4 Delce Cottages opposite the School, part of the W. S. Coles building business. I don’t know when this original Post Office ceased trading, but it became a Post Office again when the official Post Office and Shop closed in the 1990’s.

## Boots & sweets!

The Village Shop was not the only place where you could shop. Fred Rolfe was the village ‘snob’ (cobbler), working from his house at #134 Monkton Street. But one could knock on the back door and be served an array of sweets, fizzy pop drinks, single cigarettes, and treats that were particularly popular to the village children with pennies to spend (money went a lot further in those days!)

In a 1966 Isle of Thanet newspaper article, Monkton’s cobbler was featured :- “Fred was 78 (b1888), had lived in Monkton since 1919, and has been the village’s shoe-repairer for close on 60 years. Besides his soleing and heeling activities, he also stitches gloves, mends handbags and makes the occasional leather wallet and school satchel”.

## Is it a plane?!

In the 1980’s, when Manston International Airport was in its heyday, it wasn’t unusual to hear American F111 jets race across the sky. Or the Red Arrows in formation going off to an airshow or returning. And even Concorde landed at Manston and offered short trips around the Bay of Biscay, the North Sea, and even to Heathrow! There are internet videos of Concorde at Manston in the mid 1990’s. As well as emergency and day trip helicopters, the skies over Monkton have always been interesting.

## Charles & Di: Wednesday 29th July 1981!

Monkton organised its own special celebration for the “Wedding of the Century” between Prince Charles, heir to the throne, and Lady Diana Spencer. The night before the wedding, there was a grand party in the Village Hall, along the lines of a good old Music Hall, with turns by talented villagers, a hat competition and community singing. Next day everyone gathered around their TV with an estimated 750 million others globally. In the afternoon, a grand fancy-dress procession set off from Monkton Court Farm to Monkton School with horse-drawn Monkton village prince and princess (Tracy IRELAND) with attendants, and vintage cars. There followed an hour of sports on the School field – races running forwards and backwards, three-legged egg-and-spoon races etc. Then a Tea Party for villagers in the Village Hall, with “a feast of gigantic proportions”. Finally, there was a “Village Hop” and a “diversity of music flowed into the evening air. People danced, pranced, jigged and chatted in a thoroughly relaxed atmosphere. Sustenance was provided and most welcome …… so after final waltzes, sighs, tears and cheers, we rambled home, weary, but very content, having had a wonderful day of celebration.” Memorable times I’m sure, and so important for villagers and organisers to have done it when you look back on happy memories. Thank you to all those fantastic helpers and volunteers who organise events like this. Long may they continue!

## Gas explosion!

The BBC News on Monday 26th November 2007 reported:- “GAS EXPLOSION IN WORKSHOP BLAZE”. Homes were evacuated and an (400m) exclusion zone put in place after a fire broke out at a Kent farm workshop which contained gas canisters. About 30 firefighters tackled the blaze at Hoo Farm, in Monkton Road, Minster. Propane and acetylene cylinders were confirmed on site, and at least one of them exploded. (30) Firefighters spent about four hours dousing the fire. A 400m (437yds) exclusion zone was put in place and was expected to remain until Tuesday 27th November. The blaze was reported just before 1300 GMT on Monday 26th November. Crews were confronted with "a well-developed fire in a large single-storey building", Kent Fire and Rescue Service said. Twenty people were moved to safety from nearby homes, but there were no reports of any injuries. Six firefighters were expected to continue cooling the remaining gas cylinders until about midday on Tuesday 27th November.”

## Party time!

Monkton has in the past had annual seasonal fairs, on 22nd July for the sale of hogs, and the other on 11 October for the sale of toys, which contributed to local trade, a tradition dating back to the parish’s time as a market town. These would be held at “The Vicarage”, “Monkton Court Farmhouse”, or “The Croft” because of their expansive private grounds able to accommodate stalls and music, maypole dancing and races, and refreshments etc.

In Monkton Churchyard, around the years of 2006, you’d see a group of our older pupils cleaning and tidying war graves in association with a Monkton School project about the two World Wars led by Headteacher Mrs Janet Graham (Headteacher from 1990-2015). It was a subject that fired the imagination and that led to research of Monkton’s War Memorial military personnel, reading and studying the John McCrae poem “In Flanders Fields” and “The Soldier” by Rupert Brooke, and a trip to the Imperial War Museum in London.

# Credits

This site exists because of the people who have researched, written,
and shared what they know about Monkton. This page records who's
contributed so far.

## Stuart Horsburgh — Project Lead

Stuart leads this project: gathering material, interviewing villagers,
and steering what the site covers. His own contributions so far
include [Stuart's Research](history/stuarts-research/index.md) — his
folk history of Monkton, built around the
[Centenary Mile Walk of 1926](history/stuarts-research/centenary-walk/index.md),
a house-by-house guide to the village as it looked a hundred years ago,
with special stories and villagers' memories — and the churchyard survey that underpins the
[Church](church/index.md) section's clergy list, bells, war memorial,
and full record of the churchyard's graves.

## Terry Marsh

Terry has contributed two regular columns, originally written for the
Monkton News parish magazine and reproduced here: [Monkton
Memories](history/terry-marshs-memories/monkton-memories/index.md), childhood and
village-life reminiscences, and [A Farming Life](history/terry-marshs-memories/farming-life/index.md),
an account of his working life on Monkton and Thanet farms from 1976
onwards. Together they're the site's main first-hand record of what
the village was actually like to live and work in. Terry has also
shared a great deal of background information about the village.

## Thank you

A big thank you, too, to everyone else who has shared information,
memories, photographs and documents with the project — whether in a
conversation in the churchyard, over the garden fence, or by passing on
a family photo. A folk history only works because people are willing
to share what they know, and every contribution helps build a fuller
picture of Monkton's past.
